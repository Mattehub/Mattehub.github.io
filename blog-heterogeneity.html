The role of structural heterogeneity in shaping whole-brain dynamics across scales

Preprint on bioRxiv · 2025 · Read the preprint

Brain structure is not uniform: some regions are highly connected hubs, while others are more specialized. We study how this heterogeneity shapes neural dynamics from local circuits to whole-brain patterns.

Key idea: realistic structural diversity changes the stability, speed, and scale of brain dynamics compared to homogeneous models.

Using large-scale modeling and empirical structural data, we show how heterogeneity affects synchronization, variability, and multi-scale organization. These effects are strong enough to change the qualitative behavior of the model.

The results highlight why brain models need to respect regional differences to explain activity across scales, rather than relying on one-size-fits-all assumptions.
