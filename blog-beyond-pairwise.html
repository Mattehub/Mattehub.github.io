Beyond Pairwise Interactions: Charting Higher-Order Models of Brain Function

Preprint on bioRxiv · 2025 · Read the preprint

Brain network models often assume that regions interact only in pairs. This project shows that such pairwise links miss coordinated activity among groups of regions, which can carry crucial information about brain function.

Key idea: higher-order models capture patterns that cannot be reduced to the sum of pairwise interactions.

We compare multiple higher-order frameworks (rooted both in Information Theory and Topological Data Analysis) and show how they provide new insights about brain function, how they relate to reach others and compare to more traditional approaches. Our work shows when pairwise models are enough, when higher-order interactions unveil new insights, and how to interpret the added information brought by higher-order analysis.
