Higher-order and distributed synergistic functional interactions encode information gain in goal-directed learning

Preprint on bioRxiv · 2024 · Read the preprint

Learning is often described as a change in single regions, but the brain typically learns by combining information across distributed systems. Here we measure how groups of regions work together to encode information gain during goal-directed learning.

Key idea: synergy between regions predicts how much new information a participant gains.

We use higher-order interaction measures to quantify synergy and redundancy in functional signals, and relate these metrics to behavioral performance. The results show that synergy is not local; it spreads across a distributed network that shifts as learning progresses.

This provides a mechanistic link between higher-order coordination and adaptive behavior, and points to higher-order models as essential tools for studying learning in complex tasks.
